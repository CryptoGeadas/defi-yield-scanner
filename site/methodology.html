How this works

← Back to rankings Source ↗

A trust filter for stablecoin yield. It answers one question — “where can I park my stables right now, and is that yield real?” — and it treats a high APY as a red flag as often as an opportunity.

What it is (and isn’t)

This is a trust filter, not a yield hunter. Everything it shows has passed a deliberate, opinionated set of filters, and the ranking rewards durable, organic, accessible yield. It is a personal daily tool published in the open — not investment advice, and not a complete market view.

What gets shown

A pool only appears if it clears every one of these, checked at build time:

  • Stablecoin only — the pool is stablecoin-denominated.
  • Trusted protocol — its protocol is on a curated allowlist. Membership is the trust signal; anything not listed doesn’t render.
  • Mapped stables — every leg of the pool maps to a known stablecoin.
  • Supported chain — Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base, Optimism, Polygon, or Solana.
  • Real size — TVL above $1M, and not flagged as a statistical outlier. For lending pools, TVL is total deposits (what users supplied), not deposits minus loans — the same meaning as for vaults. A lending pool must also have at least $1M available to withdraw (not lent out), so a fully borrowed market never shows up.
The curation is intentional, but it currently also hides legitimate pools (for example, vault-wrapped positions). Coverage is being expanded over time — the set you see is a trusted subset, not the whole market.

Tiers

Each stablecoin has a tier, by how it is backed:

Tier 1Blue-chip, fiat-backed — USDC, USDT, DAI, and peers.
Tier 2Crypto-backed — crvUSD, GHO, LUSD, sUSDS, and similar.
Tier 3Synthetic — USDe, USR, and newer designs.

A multi-asset pool takes the tier of its riskiest leg — a USDC/USDe pool is Tier 3. The tier is the Coin factor of a pool’s level; the windows on the page are grouped by level (see Levels).

Levels

A pool’s level is how much risk it carries as a whole, not only its coin: Level 1 · Lower risk, Level 2 · Medium risk, Level 3 · Higher risk. Each pool is rated on six factors, each one Level 1, 2 or 3, and the pool takes its weakest factor. A pool is never safer than its weakest point.

CoinThe tier of its riskiest stablecoin (see Tiers). This is a ceiling: a pool holding a synthetic coin can never be Level 1.
SizeTVL (total deposits). Level 1 from $50m, Level 2 from $5m, Level 3 below.
AgeDays the pool has been live. Level 1 from 180 days, Level 2 from 30 days, Level 3 below.
YieldThe pool’s own 30-day yield (rewards taken out) as a multiple of the base rate. Level 1 up to 1.5×, Level 2 up to 2.5×, Level 3 above. A pool paying far above the market must be taking risk somewhere.
StabilityToday’s yield against its 30-day average — only rises count. Level 1 up to +30%, Level 2 up to +60%, Level 3 above. A sudden rise usually means most of the pool is lent out, or a temporary incentive (a reward campaign starting counts too). A falling yield is not a safety risk. A rise under 1 percentage point never counts.
ProtocolSet by hand per protocol (list below).

Each row shows the six factors as a strip of six segments — colour and height show each factor’s level. Open a row to see the values.

Base rate

The base rate is the market’s plain stablecoin lending rate: the 30-day average yield of Aave USDC and USDT on Ethereum. It moves with the market, so the Yield limits move with it — 1.5× means 6% when Aave pays 4%, and 9% when Aave pays 6%.

Missing data

When a factor has no data (for example no start date for a pool), it counts as Level 2 and the row says unknown, with what is missing. A gap in our data is not proof of risk, and not proof of safety.

Levels move slowly on purpose

Data refreshes every 6 hours, but a pool’s level doesn’t jump with every refresh. A pool moves down after two refreshes in a row at a worse level (about 12 hours), and up only after seven days in a row at a better level. A change to its coin’s tier or its protocol’s level moves it at once. Bad news shows fast; good news has to hold. Moves are not announced on the page. The strip always shows today’s values, so for a short time it can disagree with the pool’s level.

Protocol levels

Level 1: the current version has been live 2+ years, holds $1B+, and has had no loss of user funds. Level 2: newer or smaller, or a past incident that was fixed or repaid. Level 3: a recent loss of user funds, or a failure without a clear fix. An off-chain party (borrowers, a fund issuer) is not by itself a reason for a lower level.

Level 1Aave V3, Compound V2 / V3, SparkLend, Spark Savings, Sky, Morpho, Kamino Lend, Uniswap V3, Maple, BlackRock BUIDL, Invesco USTB, Ondo.
Level 2Aave V4, Uniswap V4, Euler V2 (2023 exploit, recoverable funds returned), Jupiter Lend, Yearn, Beefy, Aerodrome, Velodrome, Orca, Raydium, marginfi, Centrifuge, Midas, Balancer V3, Ethena.
Level 3Save (ex-Solend; 2022 oracle exploit), Balancer V2 (exploited November 2025), Usual (USD0++ depeg, January 2025).

Maple is Level 1 here but trust tier 2 in the Tokenized RWA Tracker. They answer different questions: this one asks how safe the protocol is to deposit into; the RWA tracker asks how real and simple the backing is. Both can be true.

Stable types

Beyond how risky a stablecoin is (its tier), it helps to know how it holds its value. Every pool is tagged with one type, and you can filter the rankings by it:

Fiat-backedEach token is backed by real dollars / T-bills held in reserve (USDC, USDT, DAI). The simplest kind.
Crypto-backedBacked by a surplus of crypto locked as collateral (crvUSD, GHO, LUSD). Peg held by over-collateralisation.
Yield-bearingA wrapped/staked stable that grows on its own (sUSDS, sUSDe, Maple SyrupUSDC). You hold a token that keeps earning even before you deposit it anywhere.
RWATokenised real-world assets — mostly short-term US Treasuries (BUIDL, USTB, USDY). Usually permissioned (🔒 KYC).
SyntheticHolds its peg with a strategy rather than simple reserves — e.g. a delta-neutral hedge (USDe, USDX). Higher, more novel risk.

A multi-asset pool takes the type of its riskiest leg, same as its tier.

Buckets

Within each tier, every pool is one of three kinds of place to earn:

LendSupply into a lending market (Aave, Morpho, Compound…).
LPProvide liquidity to a pool (Curve, Uniswap…).
VaultDeposit into a managed/auto-compounding vault (Yearn, a Morpho vault…).

The APY, and why we rank on “base”

Each row’s bar splits the yield in two: a solid segment for base (organic) APY — the yield the pool produces on its own — and a faint segment for reward APY, the extra incentive paid in a farm token.

We rank on base APY only. Rewards are shown but never counted, because incentive yield is temporary and often the thing that makes a headline number look better than it is. A pool that’s mostly rewards sits low, with a long faint tail — the thesis, drawn.

Liquidity-pool (LP) yield is different. A DEX pool’s APY is just its recent trading fees annualised, so it swings wildly with volume. To avoid flattering a pool during a volume spike, an LP row shows the conservative figure — the lower of its current rate and its 30-day average, with rewards taken out of both — so the headline never overstates what you’d actually earn. Rewards on an LP pool are shown in the faint segment, like everywhere else. The current spot rate is still shown when you expand the row. Lending and vault APYs are steady, so those use the live rate.

Durability — the ▲ ● ▼ flag

The trend flag compares a pool’s current APY to its 30-day average, so you can tell a durable rate from a temporary spike:

▲Spiking — currently more than 20% above its 30-day mean. The number may not last.
●Steady — within ±20% of its mean. The most trustworthy state.
▼Decaying — more than 20% below its mean. The rate is fading.

Hover any flag on the rankings to see the exact numbers spelled out.

The links

Open a row to reach where you’d actually deposit. Links come in three kinds:

Exact poolStraight to the specific pool/vault on the protocol’s own app — computed or looked up per protocol (Aave, Curve, Uniswap V3, Morpho vaults today; more over time).
Protocol appThe protocol’s own app, when an exact per-pool URL isn’t reliably derivable — you pick the market there.
NonePermissioned pools (see below) aren’t linked.

Permissioned / 🔒 KYC

Some of the largest stablecoin yield is permissioned RWA (tokenized T-bills and credit) that requires KYC. Those pools are hidden by default and revealed with the “show permissioned” toggle, always marked 🔒 KYC. They’re shown for completeness, not linked.

Money flows

Open a vault’s row to see how much money went in and out of it: the net over the last 7 and 30 days, and the biggest single exit. It shows whether people are adding to a vault or leaving it — a large outflow is easy to miss when you only look at its size.

The numbers are the vault’s own deposits and withdrawals, read once a day from the vault contract on Ethereum through SQD’s public data service. Amounts are in the vault’s stablecoin, read as dollars. Each tracked vault’s card links to its contract on Etherscan so you can check them yourself.

Money flows are shown only. They never change a pool’s risk level.

Collecting since …The vault was added recently. Numbers show once it has 7 full days. A vault not read yet says “collecting from the next daily update”.
Since …The vault has between 7 and 29 days of history, so the numbers cover the days since that date, not 30 days.
As of …The last update is over 36 hours old, or its data ends more than 2 days ago. The numbers are dimmed and run to that date.
UnavailableThe flows data could not be read for this update, or this vault’s record was damaged. No numbers are shown rather than guessed ones.
Not trackedFlows are read only for single-coin vaults on Ethereum (Morpho, Yearn, Euler). The card says why a pool is not one of them: a DEX pool, a lending market, a protocol whose flows are not read, another chain, a coin not worth $1, an older vault version, or a vault that could not be confirmed.

A day with no deposits or withdrawals shows a real $0. Missing days are never counted as $0.

How deposits work

Some rows have a Deposit button. It opens a panel where you put your stablecoins into that vault without leaving the page. You connect your own browser wallet (MetaMask, Rabby or similar) and sign every step in it. The route and the transaction come from LI.FI, a service that finds the way from your coin to the vault and builds the transaction for you to sign.

This site never holds your money, your keys or your data. Your funds go from your wallet to the vault through LI.FI’s contract, and the vault’s shares come back to your wallet. The site takes no fee. LI.FI charges its own fee (0.25% today), and the network charges gas; the quote shows both before you sign. This is not financial advice: the risk level on the row stays the same, and the panel is the same for every level.

Before anything is signed, the panel checks LI.FI’s answer against the deposit you asked for: the chain, the coin, the amount, your address, the vault’s token at the end and LI.FI’s own contract. If any of it doesn’t match, nothing is signed. When your allowance is too low, you first approve exactly the amount you deposit, never more.

Which rowsA row gets a button when the Scanner can match it to a vault LI.FI can deposit into: by the vault’s contract address (Morpho, Yearn and similar), or, for Aave’s Core markets and Spark Savings, when exactly one LI.FI vault fits the chain, the coin and the pool’s size.
No buttonPools on Solana, DEX pools (LP), KYC products, vaults LI.FI doesn’t list or can’t deposit into today, and any match the Scanner isn’t sure of. A wrong match would send money to the wrong vault, so when in doubt there is no button. Use the row’s venue link instead.
Another chainYou can pay with the same coin from another chain. LI.FI bridges it to the vault’s chain, then deposits. The panel shows the time LI.FI expects and follows the transfer until it is done.
If a bridge stops halfwayRarely, the bridge works but the deposit on the other side does not. Then you hold the coin (or another token) in your wallet on the vault’s chain, not vault shares, or the transfer is refunded to the chain you paid from. The panel says which, with links to the transactions and to LI.FI’s tracker. Nothing is lost; you can deposit again from there.
WithdrawalsNot in the panel yet. Withdraw on the vault’s own app, through the row’s venue link.

Data & freshness

Pool data comes from DefiLlama. A scheduled job refreshes the snapshot every 6 hours and commits it to the repo, so the page loads instantly with no backend — and the commit history doubles as a record of how yields drift over time.

Not investment advice. The rankings encode one person’s opinion about trust, deliberately.
